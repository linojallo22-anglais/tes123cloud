# Why Israel and Iran Can't Invade Each Other — English Short 9:16 (~1 min)

## ElevenLabs text
Israel and Iran have traded strikes again and again. But neither army can simply drive to the other country. Here's why, on the map.

They're almost a thousand miles apart. That's farther than New York to Chicago. And they don't share a single border: Jordan, Iraq, Syria or Saudi Arabia stand in the way.

Israel is about the size of New Jersey. At its narrowest, it's only nine miles wide. So it built a shield in the sky, with one layer that stops missiles in space.

Iran is almost as big as Alaska, and it's walled off by the Zagros Mountains, more than nine hundred miles long. It even hid a nuclear site three hundred feet under a mountain. To hit it, America flew B-2 bombers all the way from Missouri.

But Iran's strongest card is water: the Strait of Hormuz. It's just twenty-one miles wide, and normally about a fifth of the world's oil goes through it.

Mountains or a shield in space: which one wins? Tell me in the comments.
